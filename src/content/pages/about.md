I am a Ph.D. student in Applied and Computational Mathematics at Yale University. My Ph.D. advisors are Chairman **Yihong Wu** and Professor **Ronald Coifman**. My research has been on a few orthogonal directions, including mathematical physics, information theory, statistical learning theory, and transcendental geometry. 
Although these directions are weakly connected, I have made successful progresses in them by leveraging 1. principled mathematical mind, 2. careful and efficient numerical computations, which sometimes lead to important theoretical insights, 3. the power of agentic AI to increase the turnover rate of my idea by reducing time spent on executions such as writing proves and codes. This allows me to become an idea/insight provider and verifier, saving time on executions. 



My primary current focus is AI for mathematical discovery: developing ways for AI systems to formulate, investigate, and help establish substantive mathematical insights. 

My previous research developed foundations for XGBoost and related tree-based learning methods, with particular attention to convergence and missing covariates, and studies optimal distributions for amplitude-constrained Gaussian channels using tools from approximation theory.
Before Yale, I was a Research Analyst at the Flatiron Institute's Center for Computational Mathematics, where I worked with Leslie Greengard and Manas Rachh on fast solvers for Stokes, Helmholtz, and Lippmann–Schwinger equations. I also contributed to the [chunkIE](https://github.com/fastalgorithms/chunkie) integral-equation toolbox.
