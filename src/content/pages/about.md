I am a Ph.D. student in Applied and Computational Mathematics at Yale University. My Ph.D. advisors are Chairman **Yihong Wu** and Professor **Ronald Coifman**. 

I have worked across several orthogonal areas, including mathematical physics, information theory, statistical learning theory, and transcendental geometry. 
Across these areas, I rely on three complementary strengths: 
1. principled mathematical reasoning; 
2. careful, efficient numerical computation that can reveal theoretical structure and insights; 
3. and agentic AI tools that help me test and refine ideas more quickly by accelerating implementation, coding, and proof development. This workflow allows me to focus on generating insights and rigorously verifying results.


My primary current focus is AI for mathematical discovery: developing ways for AI systems to formulate, investigate, and help establish substantive mathematical insights. 




## Education

- **Yale University** — Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics, expected May 2027.
- **New York University** — B.A. in Mathematics and B.A. in Computer Science, 2022.

## Interests

AI for mathematical discovery, machine learning theory, statistical learning, information theory, scientific computing, numerical PDEs, and boundary integral methods.


## Previously 
My previous research developed foundations for XGBoost and related tree-based learning methods, with particular attention to convergence and missing covariates, and studies optimal distributions for amplitude-constrained Gaussian channels using tools from approximation theory.
Before Yale, I was a Research Analyst at the Flatiron Institute's Center for Computational Mathematics, where I worked with Leslie Greengard and Manas Rachh on fast solvers for Stokes, Helmholtz, and Lippmann–Schwinger equations. I also contributed to the [chunkIE](https://github.com/fastalgorithms/chunkie) integral-equation toolbox.
